Who are you beyond your age?

Jul 20, 2026

Do you think age matters?

For a long time, I believed age didn't matter to me. Of course, as I'm getting older, I do think more about my health. Because I can feel my body getting heavier day by day. And I also worried about my age when I first came to Australia. I look younger than my actual age, but I wondered if the age gap might become a barrier to making friends. But then I thought, "People probably won't ask my age anyway." I believed people simply didn't care.

Now I've been living in Australia for several years now, and my experience has been quite different. Many Australians seem surprisingly interested in my age. Even after they've already found out how old I am, some of them ask me again whenever we meet. I know they're joking about how much I look younger, but after hearing it so many times, I don't really find it happy anymore.

What's interesting is that my Korean friends—and many of my other Asian friends—rarely ask about my age. Once they know, it's simply not a topic that comes up again. So I've started wondering... Is this a cultural difference? Or have I just happened to meet people who really like joking about how young I look? Sometimes I even catch myself asking, "Why does my age seem to matter so much here?"

In Korea, age is pretty important because we have a hierarchical culture. We use different titles depending on whether someone is older or younger than us, and those titles show respect. In that context, people often ask your age when they first meet you so they know how to address you properly. After that, they usually just remember whether you're older or younger. The exact number doesn't matter anymore, so they rarely ask again.

But I've noticed that many Westerners seem to use age as a casual conversation topic. I'm sure most people don't mean any harm. It's probably just a different way of making conversation. But it made me think about how differently we see age.

I don't even keep track of my age every year. I've seen people limit their own potential simply because of their age. When I tell people I enjoy surfing, they often say, "I'm 50. Can I even start?" Or they think they're too old to start something new, study abroad, or even go on a working holiday. I always want to say, "Of course you can."

Some people say the only thing you can't do as you get older is be a child model. Yeah, I think that's true. If you want to travel the world, now is a great time to start. If you want to become an artist, just paint as much as you can. It's never too late.

Of course, you can ask me how old I am.

I just don't want people to treat me as a number. I want people to be curious about who I am. I don't want to be judged by my age. I'd rather be asked about my hobbies, what I'm passionate about, or what my childhood was like. I don't really think about age anymore. It's just a number to me.
